# Writing Tone — Mike's Voice

Adapted from SpectraSort's copy rules. Apply to game UI copy, onboarding, landing
pages, and any public-facing text.

## Section headers — terse, single word or short phrase

**Yes:** `FAST`, `SCORING`, `HOW TO PLAY`, `SETTINGS`

**No:** `THE FAST PART`, `THE SCORING PART`, `THREE WAYS TO START`

The pattern "THE ___ PART" reads as a forced editorial frame. Strip the frame. A
header earns its weight from the noun, not the wrapper.

## Em-dash discipline

Em-dashes are for asides and beat shifts inside a sentence — not for enumerations.

- ✅ "hundreds / thousands of scores"
- ❌ "hundreds — or thousands — of scores"

## Prefer functional over poetic

- ✅ "your score" · ❌ "your brilliance"
- ✅ "your best run" · ❌ "your legend"

Mike will take the workmanlike noun over the gauzy aesthetic noun. Reserve poetic
framing for the tagline / hero — never the body copy or instructions.

## Lead with the outcome, then the mechanic

- ✅ "Clear the board before the timer ends. Match three or more tiles to score."
- ❌ "Match three or more tiles to score. The timer gives you sixty seconds."

Name what the player gets, then how they get it.

## State the fact and the mechanism — no vague marketing speak

When a line conveys facts, be specific enough that the reader knows exactly what was
meant. Name the concrete problem, the change, and — when it helps — the mechanism.

- ✅ "Each combo adds 10 points; a missed tap resets it."
- ❌ "Combos keep you in the loop."

## Delete 40%, add nothing

Assume any first draft is ~40% too long. The second pass is deletion, not polish.

What gets cut:
- Category labels the reader can infer
- Reassurance lines at the end
- Any sentence restating what the adjacent line just said
- Instructions for things the UI already makes obvious

## Concrete numbers, never rounded away

`60 seconds` · `3 lives` · `10-point combo` · `390px` — specifics beat "a short timer"
or "a few lives".

## Microcopy

- Buttons: verb first — `Start`, `Retry`, `Resume`, `Share score`.
- Errors: say what happened and what to do next. No blame, no cuteness.
- Empty states: one line of direction, not a paragraph.
